Throwing Putters & Putting Putters (Speeds 2–3)

From powering off the tee on tight 250-foot wooded lines to converting pressure putts inside Circle 1, discover the putters in our personal bag.

Category Guide

Throwing Putters vs. Putting Putters

One of the most important distinctions in modern disc golf is separating your throwing putters from your putting putters. Throwing putters are molded in durable premium plastics (Star, Special Blend, DuraFlex) designed to withstand high-velocity tree impacts off the tee without warping. Putting putters, by contrast, are molded in softer, baseline chalky blends (Gateway SSS, Prodigy 300, KC Pro) to provide maximum tactile grip in humid Georgia conditions and absorb chain momentum inside Circle 1 (within 33 feet of the basket).

Putter Flight Characteristics

How low speed and high control shape short-game performance:

Speed 2 to 3

Deep Rim Aerodynamics

Deep, rounded rims resist flipping over, holding true flight paths even when thrown with high torque.

Glide 2 to 5

Lift & Float

High glide (4–5) allows floaty putts from Circle 2; low glide (2) prevents long comeback putts in wind.

Turn 0

Direct Flight Line

Putters feature neutral turn (0), maintaining a straight trajectory from release to target.

Fade 1 to 2

Gentle Finish

Minimal low-speed fade ensures the disc hits chains flat or settles gently near the pole.

Midranges & Approach Discs (Speeds 1–5)

Precision tools engineered for controlling landing zones, carving tight technical lines, and scrambling up-and-downs from the rough.

Category Guide

Scoring in the Red Zone

While distance drivers grab attention, midranges and approach discs determine your score. On courses like Rozar Park in Perry or Ace Run Hollow in Macon, these discs dominate every hole. Midranges (speeds 4–5) fly on true flight angles with minimal ground skip. Approach discs (speeds 1–4) offer specialized low-glide profiles and blunt rims built to absorb ground impact and settle beneath the basket.

Midrange & Approach Flight Mechanics

Why flight control trumps speed inside 250 feet:

Speed 1 to 5

Controlled Pace

Blunt, rounded rims slow aerodynamic forward momentum, preventing wild skips and deep forest deflections.

Glide 1 to 5

Drop or Carry

Approach discs feature low glide (1–3) to "drop and stop," while midranges (4–5) float effortlessly down the fairway.

Turn +0.5 to -3

Shot Shaping

Understable midranges turn smoothly to the right, while overstable approach molds resist any high-speed turn.

Fade 1 to 4

Pinpoint Finish

Determines whether the disc settles softly on its flight line (1) or bites aggressively into the turf (3–4).

The Discraft Buzzz is the undisputed king of straight midranges. Featuring an ultra-comfortable beadless rim, it holds whatever angle it is released on with zero surprise deviations and finishes with a subtle, gentle forward settling.

Intended Fairway Use & Shot Role
Dead-straight tee shots on par 3s; gentle drifting anhyzers; hyzer approach shots that land flat without rolling.
